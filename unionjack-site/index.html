What we do

Union Jack is a family-run British car restoration shop in San Martin, California, in business since 1988.

We take classic British cars — Jaguar, MG, Triumph, Austin-Healey, Mini and the rest — from running-but-tired to concours d'elegance standard, with every stage handled under one roof: engine and gearbox rebuilds, suspension, brakes, wiring, metalwork, paint and upholstery. John and Marcello Locascio have run the shop together since it opened. Most cars arrive from between San Francisco and Monterey Bay; a good number are shipped in from other states.

No surprises

Restoration goes wrong in predictable ways: a vague estimate, a car stripped to bare metal, then a phone call about money. We work to stop that happening.

  1. We look at the car properly

    A full assessment before any number is quoted — what it needs, what can wait, and what we would leave alone.

  2. You get an estimate and a worksheet

    Not a single figure. A written breakdown of every stage, so you can see where the money goes and say no to any part of it.

  3. We find the parts first

    The tedious bit, done up front. Warehouses, specialists and contacts built up over thirty-eight years, sometimes in the UK. Knowing a part exists before we start is what keeps a schedule honest.

  4. You see it as it goes

    Progress reviews at each stage, with photographs. Come by the shop whenever you like — the car is yours.

  5. It keeps our number afterwards

    We stay in touch with cars we have built, often for decades. If something needs attention later, call Marcello directly.

Questions owners actually ask

Straight answers, including the ones about money.

What does a full restoration cost?

A body-off restoration of a British sports car generally runs into the tens of thousands of dollars, and a concours-level car can cost more than the finished car is worth. The honest answer is that no one can quote you accurately from a photograph. We assess the car, then give you a written estimate and a stage-by-stage worksheet before any work starts, so you decide what is worth doing.

How long does a restoration take?

A full body-off restoration typically takes twelve to twenty-four months. The single biggest variable is parts: a car needing rare trim or a specific gearbox can wait months on one component. We search for parts before starting so the schedule is based on what we can actually get.

Do you take cars from outside California?

Yes. We regularly work on cars shipped in from other states and can recommend enclosed transporters. Most of our local work comes from between San Francisco and Monterey Bay, but the shop has built cars for owners across the country.

What is a rolling restoration?

A rolling restoration means the car stays usable while it is worked on in stages — brakes and suspension one winter, paint the next. It spreads the cost over years and keeps you driving. It suits a car you want to enjoy rather than show.

Do you only work on British cars?

No. British sports cars are what we are known for, but we restore and repair classic American cars and trucks too — Chevrolet, Ford, Plymouth and Buick among them — along with other European makes.

Can I visit while my car is being built?

Yes, and we would rather you did. We hold progress reviews at each stage and you are welcome at the shop during opening hours. It is your car.

Work here

We are looking for people who want to learn this properly — metal shaping, wiring looms, SU carburettors, the things that are disappearing. Experience with old British cars is welcome but not required; a genuine interest in doing careful work is. Apprenticeships available, tools provided.
