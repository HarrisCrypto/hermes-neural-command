What usually needs doing

Honest notes on where these cars go wrong, so you know what to look for before you buy one or bring it in.

  • Floors, frame rails and mountsAmerican cars hide rot under sound paint the same way British ones do. Floors, trunk pans and frame mounts need a lift and a hammer before anyone talks colour.
  • Brakes and steering age outDecades-old rubber, seized cylinders and worn steering boxes are safety work, not optional polish. We sort those before a car is signed off as a driver.
  • Wiring loomsCloth and early plastic looms get brittle. Adding circuits on top of a tired loom is how fires start; we renew what is unsafe rather than stacking adapters.
  • Body fit and panel gapsHeavy Detroit panels show every rushed repair. Metalwork and alignment come before paint, or the paint only advertises the shortcuts.

American classics questions

Straight answers, including the ones about cost.

Do you restore classic cars near San Jose?

Yes. The workshop is in San Martin, a short drive from San Jose, Morgan Hill and Gilroy. We also take cars from across the San Francisco Bay Area and Monterey Bay, and projects shipped from further afield.

Will you take a Chevrolet, Ford, Plymouth or Buick?

Yes. British sports cars are what we are known for, but classic American cars and trucks are part of regular work at Union Jack.

Can you do a rolling restoration on an American car?

Yes. Many owners prefer to keep the car usable while brakes, suspension, wiring or body sections are done in stages. We plan that with a written worksheet.

What does an American classic restoration cost?

The same rule as the British cars: nobody can quote accurately from a photograph alone. We look at the car, then give a written estimate and a stage-by-stage worksheet.
