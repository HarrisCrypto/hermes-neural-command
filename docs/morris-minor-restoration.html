What usually needs doing

Honest notes on where these cars go wrong, so you know what to look for before you buy one or bring it in.

  • Front chassis legs and crossmemberThe Minor's front chassis legs and the crossmember beneath the engine take the front suspension loads. They corrode from the inside and are the first thing to check on any car offered for sale.
  • Floors, sills and A-postsWater collects in the floors and sills, and the base of the A-post where the door hinges mount is a known weak point. Doors that drop are usually an A-post problem, not a hinge problem.
  • Traveller timber is load-bearingThe ash frame on a Traveller is part of the structure, not decoration. Sections can be spliced or the frame remade, but painting over decayed timber only buys a season.
  • Simple mechanically, which helpsRunning gear is straightforward and parts are well supported, so budget generally goes into bodywork rather than mechanical rebuilds.

Morris Minor questions

Straight answers, including the ones about cost.

Is the wood on a Morris Minor Traveller structural?

Yes. The ash framing on a Traveller carries load and is part of the body structure. Decayed timber has to be spliced or the frame remade; sealing and painting over it is a temporary measure only.

Where do Morris Minors rust first?

The front chassis legs and the crossmember under the engine, then floors, sills and the base of the A-posts. Doors that drop are usually an A-post problem rather than worn hinges.

Are Morris Minor parts still available?

Yes, the Minor is one of the better supported classics for parts, which keeps mechanical work relatively predictable. Most of the cost in a Minor restoration is bodywork.

Do you restore Morris Minor vans and pick-ups?

Yes. The light commercials come through the shop and have their own structural quirks around the rear body and load area.
