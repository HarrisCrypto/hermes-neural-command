Austin-Healey restoration

Union Jack restores Austin-Healeys — the 100, 100-6, 3000 and Sprite — at its workshop in San Martin, California. A big Healey restoration is structural work before it is cosmetic, because the sills and rear shroud usually decide the size of the job.

What usually needs doing

Honest notes on where these cars go wrong, so you know what to look for before you buy one or bring it in.

  • The sills are structural, not trimOn a big Healey the sills and their inner members tie the chassis together. Corroded sills mean doors that will not shut and a body that flexes. Replacing them properly means supporting the shell before anything is cut out.
  • Aluminium shroud against steelThe rear shroud is aluminium where much of the surrounding structure is steel. Where the two meet, moisture drives galvanic corrosion that bubbles paint from underneath. It has to be separated, treated and isolated, not filled.
  • Chassis rails take a beatingA big Healey sits low. Decades of grounding out leaves flattened rails and crushed outriggers, which is worth measuring before any panel is fitted.
  • Sprites rot from the insideThe Sprite is a monocoque, so floors, inner sills and front inner wings are all doing structural work. On a Bugeye the one-piece front end and its hinges are a job in themselves.

Austin-Healey questions

Straight answers, including the ones about cost.

Are Austin-Healey sills structural?

Yes. On the 100, 100-6 and 3000 the sills and inner sill members carry load and tie the chassis together. Rotten sills are a structural fault, not a cosmetic one, and the shell must be supported before they are cut out or the door gaps will never come back.

What does an Austin-Healey 3000 restoration cost?

A full body-off restoration of a big Healey runs well into the tens of thousands of dollars, and a concours car can exceed its own market value. We assess the car first, then give a written estimate with a stage-by-stage worksheet so you can decide what is worth doing.

Why does the paint bubble around the rear shroud?

The rear shroud is aluminium meeting steel structure. Moisture between the two causes galvanic corrosion that lifts paint from underneath. Filling it hides the problem for a season; the panels have to be separated, treated and isolated.

Do you work on Sprites as well as the big Healeys?

Yes. Bugeye and later Sprites come through regularly. They are monocoques, so floors, inner sills and inner wings are structural, and a small car can still need a full restoration.
